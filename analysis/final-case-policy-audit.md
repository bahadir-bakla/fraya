## Authority and scope

1. **B** controls the task, supplied call variables, exact mock fidelity and builder-only generation. **TJ** controls exact fixture bodies/statuses; **S1** controls transport, field names and request/response semantics. Business prose must not silently alter the wire contract.
2. Use **A8** as the consolidated business baseline, with topic-specific approved amendments. A8:16 explicitly says it has not incorporated every 7 October decision. **E9:21-37** explicitly says to build to the email decisions over A8, especially out-of-scope handling and production card numbers. Read each email's internal timestamp and bank correction, not just its filename: Selin's 15:02 correction and Deniz's 17:48 acknowledgment supersede Deniz's 10:14 mistaken two-calming-replies recap.
3. **M10** is the latest business discussion, but only changes/clarifies topics actually agreed there: currency names, another-card ordering, never-issued statements and closing-day amounts. Its early suggestions are not decisions: shortened "Sterlin", skipping another-card selection, substituting a future date for an issued statement and using ordinary statement debt on closing day are rejected later in the same conversation. Closing-day opening wording has an expressly accepted alternative; amount selection does not.
4. **M7** resolves earlier proposals within that meeting: third silence ends the call; three calming replies; once per calendar year per card; joint-card impact must precede the confirmation question. Its early main-menu-for-silence/one-calming-reply remarks are superseded within the meeting.
5. **D2**, **I9**, **Q5** and **F** are historical/contextual evidence. I9:7 warns it is not "how it should be". The PDF's export metadata is not a policy approval date. Examples **EX** are narrow acceptance evidence, not a license to hard-code customers. EX5's main-menu exit conflicts with the generic third-party IVR-login exit; preserve that distinction explicitly.
