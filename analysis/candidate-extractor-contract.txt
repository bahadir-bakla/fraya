1. High: scalar grammar loses different product amounts, dates and statuses.

Witness speech: "I will pay 230 for the credit card on October 16 and 420 for the personal loan on October 19." All remaining event fields keep their advertised defaults. A grammar-conforming projection kind=proposal, amount=230, date=2026-10-16, scope=all, products=[credit_card,personal_loan] produces both proposals as 230/2026-10-16. A last-clause projection amount=420/date=2026-10-19 produces both as 420/2026-10-19. Leaving amount/date empty asks for a date and creates an IDs-only fragment; it does not parse separate facts from raw.

These projections are explicitly hypothetical authored extractor outputs, not observed LLM behavior. No legal scalar event preserves both tuples. Likewise, speech proposing card 330/Oct16, saying the loan arrears are already paid, and disputing the car debt has no lossless single kind. A proposal projection with all three products makes all three proposals 330/Oct16, losing paid/dispute. No promise request is issued yet in these compound probes; a separate confirmation would still be required.

Cause: lines 118-126 expand one scalar across targets, lines 137-168 apply one date, and lines 347-350 apply one status to all targets. Product references identify targets but cannot attach separate decisions. Raw fallback handles security/keywords, not per-product payment clauses.

Smallest generic repair: explicitly support heterogeneous utterances by advertising and consuming per-product decision records, or ask targeted clarifications before constructing proposals. Never broadcast a scalar tuple onto different clauses. Do not repair this with unadvertised items injected only by tests.

2. High: unrelated product updates erase or leak a pending correction.

Start with validated pending card 460/Oct16 and loan 730/Oct16, neither recorded. A faithful correction event with raw="Wait, for the credit card I will pay 230 on", products=[credit_card], amount=230, complete=false creates fragment.ids=[fictional-rill-card], fragment.amount=230, while preserving old proposals and invalidating confirmation.

Next, a separate complete loan event amount=420/date=2026-10-19/products=[personal_loan] globally removes the unfinished card fragment and returns to confirm. The readback contains old card 460/Oct16 plus loan 420/Oct19. A following confirm requests card 460/Oct16 for writing even though its correction was never completed.

The second variant is stronger: the unrelated loan event specifies date=2026-10-19 but amount="". Policy borrows the card fragment's 230, changes loan 730 to 230, clears the card fragment and reads both back. A following yes again requests the old card 460.

Cause: lines 367-369 restrict fragment target selection when refs is empty, but lines 370-373 use fragment amount/date regardless of explicit unrelated refs; line 376 removes the fragment unconditionally. This is a deterministic state/provenance defect with faithfully targeted events. A separate yes exists; the defect is loss of the unresolved correction and cross-product facts.

Smallest repair: consume fragment fields only for the matching product set and clear only matching completed fragments. Keep unresolved corrections as a confirmation/write gate while preserving other product proposals.

3. High: support phase bypasses the incomplete-correction guard.

A one-card verified account receives hardship and enters phase=support. The next event is kind=correction, amount=230, date=2026-10-16, complete=false, products=[credit_card], raw="I can pay 230 on October 16, wait, let me correct that". It produces proposal 230/Oct16, phase=confirm and no fragment. A subsequent yes requests a promise for that tuple.

Cause: support lines 335-340 send proposal/partial/correction directly into propose before the general partial handler at lines 353-360. The complete=false correction is therefore not retained. This does not demonstrate absence of readback or a separate yes; it demonstrates that an explicitly unfinished correction becomes eligible without completion.

Smallest repair: handle partial/correction/complete=false before phase-specific proposal or consent branches, including support. Preserve the fragment, then require completed values, a fresh readback and a separate yes.

4. Medium: security loses independent administrative questions.

A truthful other event includes the normalized DOB answer and ai=true/scam=true in the same speech. It requests verify without answering either question. After the mocked successful DOB result, the literal dialogue is only "What are the last four digits of the phone number on file?" The AI/scam responses are absent.

Cause: lines 253-257 compute a local prefix; security line 325 routes to verify; line 415 speaks the prefix only when route=speak. No pending administrative response survives the tool hop.

Smallest repair: persist the flags until their computed response is spoken, while preserving the primary security answer.

Smallest generic source-guidance addition:
