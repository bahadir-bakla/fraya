PLANNING-ONLY / NOT RUNNABLE
These cases are not yet runnable Freya public-test-set format. This is a planning JSON object, not the runner's list of public case dictionaries. No synthetic id is registered with Freya practice sessions. No live tool, persona or simulation has been called. An adapter and local mock are required; some proposed or ambiguous cases have deliberately partial oracles.
Each case starts with a fresh local session, empty ledger and reset counters. Never carry promises, notes, wrong-answer counters or fault counters from another case.
All SYN ids are planning-only local identifiers, never Freya-registered fixtures.
Custom fixture registration is UNDOCUMENTED in the six inspected sources. A new --cases JSON does not create practice-session fixtures; run_cases.py asks the remote service for registered case IDs.
No network calls, no source-tree edits, no live credentials, no executed bank/agent tests.

EXPECTATION BASIS
explicit_kit: Directly required by the cited brief, API contract or runner/scorer behavior.
derived_from_explicit_kit: Fixture-specific arithmetic or observable consequence of cited requirements; fixture values are synthetic, not bank constants.
proposed_extension: Suggested adversarial input, trust boundary, recovery choice or local harness property absent from the six sources; not an official rule or a pass/fail requirement until adopted.
explicit_kit_with_proposed_harness: Expected reaction is explicitly sourced; deterministic input/fault/interruption delivery requires a proposed local harness feature.
explicit_contract_with_proposed_recovery: Only failed-action semantics and selected tool arguments are sourced; recovery or close outcome is not defined and is left open.

[backend/explicit_kit] Every tool uses the allocated runtime customer_id for this call. [A-vars]
[backend/explicit_kit] A completed call has exactly one close_call, as its last tool, with a valid brief outcome/reason pairing; no later tool calls. [A46-47, O-close]
[backend/explicit_kit] get_account and any create_promise occur only after both security questions have passed. [A11, A16-17, O-account]

PARAMETRIC USE WITH FUTURE BRIEFS
1. Extract the current brief into a policy object: verification fields/order, accepted identity confirmation phrases, wrong-answer budget/hints, calendars/holidays, late-band ranges, deadline business-day offsets, per-band persuasion budgets, product floors, callbacks, confirmation/retry/closing and transfer precedence.
2. Substitute fixture references to policy values. F means that product's policy floor; P means that product's current past_due; D means add_business_days(date(now), its band's offset, its active holiday calendar), excluding today from the count.
3. Generate boundary fixtures from policy values: band lower/upper and adjoining values; F, P, a value inside [F,P], F minus the chosen fixture currency increment; D and next business day after D; callback lower/upper and times equal to/just after now.
4. Holiday lists, offsets, attempt counts, queue names, literal required phrases, currencies and current customer data belong to the active brief/fixture, not to permanent builder code. Explicit numeric examples here only document the current kit.
5. Keep A/B source precedence explicit: B inheritance applies A only when B does not replace it. Re-evaluate note suppression, aggregate confirmation, clipping and closing precedence when future briefs change.
6. Maintain each oracle's source-clause provenance and basis. Changes to a future brief should regenerate derived dates and amounts and invalidate affected oracles, rather than patching hardcoded customer outcomes.
7. Treat ambiguous gaps as owner questions or partial checks. Do not convert a proposed raw memo trust rule, timeout/idempotency strategy, overpayment limit or missing-field fallback into an official bank policy without a new source.
8. For conversion to the public shape supply id/brief/family/level/title/variables/identity/products/script/expected/checks; materialize concrete ledger arrays and conversational checks. Runner-created sessions are obtained by registered case id, so a new JSON alone does not provision a case.
9. Use a local mock with independent per-product faults, split/correction turn control and an event trace. Do not invoke run_cases.py here: it creates remote personas, calls practice/simulation APIs and writes cache/results.
10. Check backend ledger contents and full trace/transcript separately. score.py sorts collections and rounds amounts and only partially checks order; add local checks for confirmation-before-create, retries, exactly-one-close, note timing and identity disclosure.
11. Replay delivery styles and repetitions without changing intended case facts. Diagnose latency separately from policy correctness; the runner's ten-second warning is not a bank SLA.

KNOWN POLICY/HARNESS GAPS
- A26 permits naming the floor again at attempt 2 while A31 prohibits repeating an already-refused offer; identical floor/date combinations require interpretation.
- A27 does not specify handling an offered amount above past_due, and current brief bands do not define behavior for days_late outside 1..45.
- The briefs specify create_promise retry exactly, but not a recovery/close-outcome policy for failed schedule_callback or transfer_to_agent.
- No raw memo intake/override format, trust hierarchy, timeout ambiguity, idempotency key or context-injection handling is defined by the six files.
- B8/B9 product notes and B10 no-notes-on-transfer must be reconciled across the whole call. Deferring notes is a proposed implementation technique, not a quoted requirement.
- The API product schema lacks required fields/domain bounds; do not assume that implies permission to invent absent financial facts.

BOUNDED-CORPUS LIMITATIONS
- Bounded initial corpus of 50 scenarios, not exhaustive rule or cross-product coverage. Coverage entries identify relevant checks, not proof of implementation conformance.
- Not independently exercised here: all-B-products-paid closing, same-call first-failure-then-success A retry, voluntary hardship promise, invalid callback service failure, above-full offers, out-of-band delinquency, missing financial fields and lost-response/idempotency behavior. Some precedents or gaps are documented in source catalog/review_gaps, but are not additional cases.
- Custom synthetic fixture registration is undocumented in the six inspected sources. Supplying this JSON via --cases would not register SYN ids with practice sessions.
- No latency pass/fail SLA is invented. Runtime now, callback boundaries and split-turn input are exercised; runner latency/repetition behavior is documented as harness evidence.
- Raw memo semantics and credential-handling attacks are proposed extensions. All identities and foreign-token markers in this corpus are synthetic; no real credentials are included.
