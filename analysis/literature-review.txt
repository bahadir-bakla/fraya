ASSESSMENT
[inference] The best-supported design direction is constrained compilation: a provenance-linked policy representation and normalized tool contract, deterministic guards for consequential actions, a shared ordered product ledger, and separately derived state and temporal checks. A few conversation phases can realize the flow while preserving state. Automatic extraction completeness and runtime interruption behavior remain the main unresolved engineering questions.

Coordinate implementation and cases with their existing owners.

RANKED ACTIONABLE RECOMMENDATIONS
1. R01 (P0) [inference] Compile briefs into an evidence-linked policy representation before generating the agent.
Action: Extract atomic rules, definitions, tables, exceptions, precedence and amendments into a typed intermediate representation. Each record should carry original source spans, scope (call/product), conditions, obligations, numeric/calendar parameters and matching tool-schema fields. Resolve B-over-A and memo-over-policy precedence explicitly. Preserve unresolved conflicts rather than silently inventing a rule.
Competition reason: This gives the generic builder a place to detect skipped rules and separates reusable compiler behavior from brief-specific values.
Evidence: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14345v1#page=4; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.13050v1#page=3 (S02, S10).
Official grounding: G01, G03, G05; see exact local locators above.
Proposed acceptance criterion: Every normative source clause is covered by an IR record or explicitly accounted for; every parameter and tool enum traces to the input; amendment resolution has a provenance trail. Different values, identity questions and outcome labels require no source-code change.
Uncertainty: No reviewed paper demonstrates complete automatic extraction of this competition's unseen, amended bank briefs. This is the central engineering hypothesis, not a solved literature result.

2. R02 (P0) [inference] Use a deterministic policy kernel for consequential decisions.
Action: Generate calendar arithmetic, amount checks, retry counters, identity gates, per-product attempts, permissible tool arguments and closing reduction as code or guards. Let the language model extract typed user events and render required speech; let code determine legal transitions and writes. Gate debt information until identity completes and success claims until the backend result permits them.
Competition reason: A correct-looking dialogue still fails if one date, product, reason or tool ordering is wrong.
Evidence: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045#page=8; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14345v1#page=4 (S01, S02).
Official grounding: G02, G03, G04, G07; see exact local locators above.
Proposed acceptance criterion: For a supplied typed event, the next legal action set and policy state are reproducible. Boundary calculations use the call's now and extracted calendar; invalid actions are rejected before execution.
Uncertainty: Natural-language interpretation remains fallible. Code guards do not prove the parser, transcription or rule extraction correct.

3. R03 (P0) [inference] Track proposed, confirmed and recorded promises separately and defer note writes.
Action: Maintain an ordered product ledger with proposed amount/date, confirmation revision, recorded result, failure count and unresolved reason. A correction invalidates the relevant confirmation snapshot. Commit only confirmed values, retain successful product writes on later failures, and never retry a successful write. Keep prospective notes in local state until a non-transfer closing path is fixed; then write notes and close. Transfer paths discard pending notes and unrecorded agreements.
Competition reason: Brief B forbids notes even before a later transfer and distinguishes recorded promises from verbal agreements; eager note writes create an irreversible policy breach.
Evidence: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045#page=25; https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/agents-handoffs/ (S01, S08).
Official grounding: G04, G05, G06, G07; see exact local locators above.
Proposed acceptance criterion: A mock run with one successful product, one failed product and a subsequent transfer keeps the successful promise, creates no notes and selects transfer closure. No product is omitted and no successful promise is duplicated.
Uncertainty: The finalization boundary and interruption behavior must be checked by the runtime/exporter owner. Deferral is a proposed solution, not a verified Freya behavior. A transfer arriving while final writes execute remains a runtime race to audit.

4. R04 (P0) [inference] Build the oracle from the source requirements through a separate implementation path.
Action: Have a checker independently read source spans and compile expected records and invariants; do not import the runtime's result-producing helpers as the expected-answer calculator. Combine final-state checks with tool-event ordering and transcript obligations. Give any language-model judge explicit rule IDs and evidence spans, then retain its rationale and uncertainty.
Competition reason: The runtime and checker can agree on the same wrongly extracted holiday, threshold or precedence. State equality alone also misses disclosure, confirmation and honest failure reporting.
Evidence: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045#page=4; https://people.cs.rutgers.edu/~uli/cs673/papers/EvaluationMultiVersionProgramming86.pdf#page=15 (S01, S09).
Official grounding: G02, G04, G06, G07; see exact local locators above.
Proposed acceptance criterion: An intentionally wrong calendar or confirmation gate is rejected by the checker even if runtime state looks internally consistent. Final-state, temporal-policy and speech checks report separately.
Uncertainty: Different models or prompts do not establish statistical independence. Source-based independent checking reduces one form of common error but cannot eliminate all shared interpretations.

5. R05 (P1) [inference] Measure whether the oracle detects realistic policy mutations.
Action: Seed targeted local mutants that bypass confirmation, mishandle holidays, alter retry/attempt bounds, suppress an unresolved-product note, lose product order, remove transfer precedence or assert recorded success after a failed tool. Evaluate whether existing tests reject them. Separate uncovered, unreachable and behaviorally equivalent mutants.
Competition reason: Rule and line coverage can look complete while a checker never notices missing obligations.
Evidence: https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/mutation-effectiveness-fse2014.pdf#page=6; https://vuir.vu.edu.au/33046/1/TSEmt.pdf#page=11 (S07, S06).
Official grounding: G02, G03, G04, G06; see exact local locators above.
Proposed acceptance criterion: The local test owner reports killed and surviving mutants by source obligation, and a passing baseline fails when each selected critical fault is injected.
Uncertainty: Mutation scores do not predict a hidden customer pass rate; policy omission and speech-understanding faults need dedicated operators.

6. R06 (P1) [inference] Use metamorphic checks with explicit preconditions.
Action: Test opaque-ID renaming with consistent backend remapping; table/prose rewrites and reordered independent paragraphs; equivalent amount/date wording; and irrelevant digressions that do not change policy-relevant facts. Compare canonical bank results and obligations, not verbatim transcripts. Treat corrections and reordered products as controlled changes where the expected answer may change.
Competition reason: Final material can change form, and final customers preserve facts while changing delivery. These transformations test generic parsing and state continuity.
Evidence: https://vuir.vu.edu.au/33046/1/TSEmt.pdf#page=11; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045#page=5 (S06, S01).
Official grounding: G01, G03, G05, G08; see exact local locators above.
Proposed acceptance criterion: Each relation states why semantics are preserved and which output is compared. A paragraph-order relation excludes chronology-dependent amendments. A date-shift relation, if added, shifts all temporal data and preserves weekday/calendar structure.
Uncertainty: Brief B product permutation is not an invariance: first unresolved non-paid product and negotiation order can change. Extra refusals or post-confirmation corrections are not harmless paraphrases.

7. R07 (P1) [inference] Evaluate repeated consistency and simulator fidelity together.
Action: Keep semantic scenario facts fixed across style variants and reruns. Require customer facts and behavioral attributes to remain faithful to the intended scenario; flag simulator errors separately. Track per-case triples, rule failures, product count and style, alongside single-run success. Reserve independent held-out practice variations for candidate selection.
Competition reason: The final counts all-three-pass customers. One green run cannot justify reliability, and simulator drift can distort diagnosis.
Evidence: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045#page=5; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07982v1#page=10; https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05444v1#S5 (S01, S04, S05).
Official grounding: G02, G08; see exact local locators above.
Proposed acceptance criterion: A customer passes a local triple only after three valid passing trajectories. Simulator failures remain visible rather than being silently discarded; official scorer results remain authoritative.
Uncertainty: Synthetic personas are stress tests, not proof of human realism. Three successes are a selection observation, not a precise estimate of true reliability. Repeats across dates/styles need stratified reporting, not an unjustified IID assumption.

8. R08 (P1) [inference] Give each conversation phase scoped instructions and shared authoritative state.
Action: Start with a few phases such as opening, identity, account/negotiation, confirmation/recording and closure, plus a common event dispatcher for questions and transfers. Carry all counters and product state across phase transitions. Accumulate partial numeric answers and apply corrections before emitting completed typed values; resume the pending question after permitted answers back.
Competition reason: Briefs require one-at-a-time identity questions and natural customer interruptions, while long silent turns can lose calls.
Evidence: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14345v1#page=6; https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/workflows/ (S02, S08).
Official grounding: G03, G04, G05, G08; see exact local locators above.
Proposed acceptance criterion: Phase changes preserve identity status, remaining attempts, candidate values and committed records. A question back does not reset progress, consume an attempt incorrectly or trigger a premature write.
Uncertainty: There is no measured basis here for a specific optimal node count. Physical turn detection and Freya route semantics belong to the runtime owner's validation.

9. R09 (P1) [inference] Bound build-check-repair search by the competition clock.
Action: Do source/IR/schema checks and local deterministic checks before an authorized live practice run. Repair the highest-impact diagnosed defect, rerun only when a new change justifies it, and retain the last valid candidate. Record each model call's purpose and timing. Reserve time to finalize the result and trace; use local tests to avoid spending the budget on repeated full simulations.
Competition reason: One simulation runs at a time, practice runs can take 3-11 minutes, and all work must fit the 30-minute cap.
Evidence: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.08435v1#page=5; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.13050v1#page=3 (S03, S10).
Official grounding: G01, G08; see exact local locators above.
Proposed acceptance criterion: The builder uses elapsed-time checks and a bounded retry count, always produces a trace, and does not schedule a test that would consume its finalization reserve.
Uncertainty: ADAS's 25-30 search iterations and FlowMind's human-feedback improvements do not transfer directly. Optimal time allocation must be measured locally.

10. R10 (P2) [unverified_idea] Use a second extraction view to find omitted or conflicting rules.
Action: Compare a source-order rule extraction with a separate extraction organized around tool preconditions, outcomes and forbidden actions. Ask the checker to identify disagreements by source span rather than vote on a preferred answer. Maintain a capability registry for requirements that cannot yet be represented.
Competition reason: Raw bank emails, memos and code lists may express exceptions indirectly or amend earlier rules.
Evidence: https://people.cs.rutgers.edu/~uli/cs673/papers/EvaluationMultiVersionProgramming86.pdf#page=17; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14345v1#page=9 (S09, S02).
Official grounding: G01, G03, G06; see exact local locators above.
Proposed acceptance criterion: A deliberately omitted exception or contradictory amendment is surfaced with both source spans before workflow generation.
Uncertainty: This has no competition-specific measured result. Run it only if bounded cost and extraction-recall improvements justify the extra model call.

PROPOSED ARCHITECTURE
1. [inference] Input normalization: Brief/amendment source spans and an API contract with fields, enums, return values and side effects. (R01).
2. [inference] Policy compilation: Typed rules with call/product scope, precedence, parameters, required speech, forbidden actions and unresolved conflicts. (R01, R10).
3. [inference] Guarded call state: Deterministic calendar and amount logic, identity and retry state, ordered product ledger, confirmation snapshots and allowed tool actions. (R02, R03).
4. [inference] Conversation realization: Scoped phase instructions; typed interpretation of fragments/corrections; question answering and resumption; shared authoritative state. (R08).
5. [inference] Independent verification: Source-derived expected records, temporal event checks, speech obligations, policy mutations, validated metamorphic relations and simulator-fidelity annotations. (R04, R05, R06, R07).
6. [inference] Bounded repair and delivery: Diagnosed fixes within the elapsed-time budget, retained last valid candidate and full build trace. (R09).
This describes interfaces and responsibilities for the builder owners. It does not specify or verify Freya export syntax, generate a test corpus or propose an alternative runtime.

INTERPRETATION CAUTIONS
[inference] Policy extraction, runtime and oracle must not share one unexamined interpretation of a brief. Distinct model names or prompts are not evidence of statistical independence.
  Evidence: S09: https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.1986.6312924; related recommendations R04, R10.
[inference] Database equality is necessary but not sufficient where permission, disclosure, ordering and truthful speech are scored. Keep these failure classes inspectable.
  Evidence: S01: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045; related recommendations R04; official grounding G02, G04, G07.
[inference] Repeated success should be recorded per scenario. Cubing an aggregate single-run pass rate is not a justified estimate of the official all-three score: scenario heterogeneity and correlated trials matter.
  Evidence: S01: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12045; related recommendations R07; official grounding G02.
[inference] A policy-preserving transformation requires explicit preconditions. Product order, a new refusal, chronology-dependent amendments or a post-confirmation correction can change the correct outcome.
  Evidence: S06: https://vuir.vu.edu.au/33046/1/TSEmt.pdf; related recommendations R06; official grounding G05, G08.
[inference] Persona labels and more nodes do not establish coverage or realism. Prefer observable scenario fidelity and specific state-preservation checks.
  Evidence: S05: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05444v1; S08: https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/workflows/; related recommendations R07, R08.
[unverified_idea] A second rule-extraction view may reveal omissions, but its benefit and cost need measurement before it consumes the final-run budget.
  Evidence: S09: https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.1986.6312924; related recommendations R10.
